# Posetrak History

Why another motion tracking project?

Posetrak stems from my background: professionally I have been working as SW engineer with images, graphics, and cameras for more than 30 years, and most of that time I have practiced aikido as my primary hobby. In summer 2025 I started to look for ways to capture aikido tehcniques, both for visualization and and analysis purposes.  Initially I looked at using many of the existing solutions both open source projects like Freemocap and Pose2sim as well as some of the then emerging AI based commerfcial services (I intentionally left the high end commercial solutions like Optitrak, Rokoko etc. out; this was and is a hobby project so my budget was limited).

Unfortunately none of these produced results that fulfilled my needs. In fact, aikido turned out to be quite extreme test for motion capture capabilities. As a martial art, it is always practiced with a partner but most of the affordable motion tracking solutions support capturing only a single person. The principle in most of the techniques is that tori (the person applying an aikido technique) guides uke (the person to whome the technique is applied) into a circular around herself at very close distance. This makes trackign the performers hard and there is usually no camera angle from which teh persons don't occlude each other; usually both occlude different parts of each other at same time and the occlusions change multiple times per second. Many of the techniques are wrist locks where one person grabs another hand - obviously and tracking is what matters here but the pose detectors have difficlties in distinguishing which part belongs to which person. And speaking of locks: the techniques do not target just wrist but force the whole joint chain from wist through elbow, shoulder etc. up to spine into an extreme pose, which means that without careful parameterization, the IK solver is continuously very close to its singularities.

I first started to write small tools to integrate the existing tools to my pipeline and help in problems they didn't solve. I created a Blender plugin to edit the raw pose detectin data, scripts use OpemSim's IK solver with Blender armatures and to export OpenSim solutions to other animation tools as BVH files. These helped me to get the motion data into the tools I wanted to use, but the large amount of outliers and noise still caused problems - movements were very jerky when one or more cameras lost visibility to key body parts. For that reason I started to look ways to improve temporal stability with filtering. I first attempted to smooth trajectories of individual keypoints with Kalman filter while still running inverse kinematics every frame to get the skleton pose, but eventually I combined these operations.

That is the key idea behind Posetrak's solver: Every frame it directly updates the skeleton pose from the 2D keypoint detections obtained from camera frames using a massive unscented Kalman filter. It does not do traditional triangularization and IK expect to initialize the first frame (and even that is not mandator, the filter usually converges quite fast even without this). This has many benefits: The probabilistic framework makes outlier detection quite robust, it is possible to adjsut temporal smoothness vs. accurate reaction to quick movements and the filter can use detections even if some keypoints are visible only in singel camera at a give frame. In fact, it is even possible to use cameras with different frame rates.

Downside of this is that the filter is computationally quite heavy. I wrote the first version in Pyhton like my existing tooling but switched to C++ soon. Still, it is not as fast as I would like it to be.

Eventually the project started to diverge too much from its origins and I started to create my own pipeline coordination too. Earlier I had stored all my data in plan text files in a structure that was largely compatible with Pose2Sim. I migrated all this into a relational database and created both GUI and command lien tools for Posetrak.

## Posetrak now

Posetrak now largely achieves original goals I had when I started this journey - I am able to capture pair and multi-person aikido practice, with equipment that is affordable for a serious hobbyist. I have successfully used tried it to capture many other activities as well. It is certainly still a tool developed for my needs - polishing it into a product is another hurdle.

In future I hope to still enhance Posetrak's solver and improve usability. I also want to add some marker-based motion capture capabilities into it; my immediate use case is to improve tracking of props like weapons. Animal motion tracking is another thing I plan to look at (my wife works as a professional animal trainer in film industry, I woudl love to do some  mocap work with her!)

Two things that are not high in my priority lsit are single camera and face mocap. Due to the reasons I explained above, I don't think that single camera mocap for my main use case (aikido) is feasible before the AI models ahve good material on it in their training corpus (and lack of that is the main reason why Posetrak exists). WHo knows about future, but for now I plan to focus on multi-camera solutions.
